## 1. ID remaps and de-duplications

**Headline finding: the spine's per-agent numeric ID blocks (§5) worked as designed.** Across 212
business requirements, 15 NFRs, 50 risk-register entries, 46 assumptions, and 227 edge cases spanning
twelve independently-drafted parts, **zero ID collisions were found.** This is a direct validation of
the manager's decision to pre-allocate ID blocks before dispatching agents (spine.md, written
"BEFORE any agent was dispatched," per its own header) — the smoke-test failure mode that motivated
the spine (orphaned IDs, duplicated requirements, an incomplete-by-construction assumptions register)
did not recur at the ID-allocation level. The remap work that *was* needed was semantic, not
numeric — one true requirement merge, one requirement rewrite to stop re-deriving another domain's
evidentiary fact, and one risk retirement.

### 1.1 The one requirement merge: BR-303 (A3) ↔ BR-802 (A8) → single ID BR-303

- **A3's BR-303** text: "The award record **is** A8's selection record — one requirement, not two:
  authority status, safety snapshot, insurance evidence, gate version, who was excluded and why, every
  bid with time, winner, rule."
- **A8's BR-802** text: "Capture at award an immutable selection record: authority status, safety
  snapshot, insurance evidence, gate applied, bidders excluded and why, basis of award."
- **Verification performed:** field-by-field comparison. Authority status ✓ both. Safety snapshot ✓
  both. Insurance evidence ✓ both. Gate applied/version ✓ both (A3 says "gate version," A8 says "gate
  applied" — same fact, different phrasing). Exclusions with reasons ✓ both. A3 additionally specifies
  "every bid with time, winner, rule" — a superset of A8's "basis of award," not a conflict.
- **Verdict:** A3's merge claim is correct. A8's content is fully subsumed by A3's BR-303 wording; no
  information is lost by retiring BR-802.
- **Canonical ID: BR-303**, kept in the Auction & Bidding domain (§8.3 of the BRD) because the
  award-time artifact is generated by the auction engine, not the liability domain. A8's §8.9
  (BRD §8.9, formerly A8's own document) shows BR-802 explicitly marked **RETIRED, merged into §8.3's
  BR-303**, with the reasoning reproduced in place rather than silently deleted, so a reader arriving
  at A8's requirement list from an old reference does not hit a dead ID with no explanation.
- **Every other requirement that referenced BR-802 by name:** none found. A7's BR-706 ("Award record
  preserves safety/eligibility data at award...") describes the same underlying content from a
  regulatory-citation angle but was never IDed as BR-802 or claimed as the same requirement by its
  authoring agent — it is cross-referenced to BR-303 in the assembled document as a citation, not
  merged, since A7 itself never claimed identity with A8's BR-802.

### 1.2 The one requirement rewrite: A8's BR-806, to stop re-deriving A2's BR-221/224

- **Problem:** A2's BR-221 ("Re-verify at pickup that the driver and equipment physically presenting
  match the tuple bound at dispatch") and A8's original BR-806 ("Verify at pickup that driver, tractor,
  MC match the awarded carrier; open a case on mismatch") both claimed the verification act itself.
  A2's own text explicitly states the intended split: "A2 owns identity/eligibility... A8 owns fraud
  typology and consequence on this evidence" — but A8's BR-806 as drafted crossed back into A2's
  territory instead of consuming its output.
- **Fix applied:** BR-806 rewritten to start from "on receipt of §8.2's BR-221/224 mismatch signal,"
  removing the re-verification clause entirely and replacing A8's acceptance condition from "mismatch
  blocks pickup, opens a case" (which also silently duplicated A4's BR-406 blocking behaviour) to "a
  classified case outcome" (double-brokering / identity theft / disclosed substitution). Full reasoning
  reproduced in the BRD at §8.9, directly under the rewritten requirement, so the edit is auditable
  against the original.
- **No ID change** — BR-806 keeps its number; only its content changed. This is recorded here as a
  content remap distinct from the BR-303/802 ID remap above.

## 2. Orphans

**Full scope statement, reproduced from the BRD itself (§8.12) because it matters for how this section
should be read:** the twelve parts contain roughly 250 prose cross-reference pointers. The assembler
resolved the ones named explicitly in the task brief with full rigor, and resolved a further
representative sample across every domain while assembling each of §8.1–§8.11. **A full line-by-line

Four genuine or partial orphans surfaced during the pass actually performed:

1. **A1 BR-106** ("→ A6/A9" — payment-reliability signal shown to bidders pre-bid). **True orphan.**
   Neither A6 nor A9 contains a requirement to render this. Nothing currently implements what BR-106
   depends on.
2. **A1 BR-108** ("derived → A8" — open non-payment dispute blocks shipper re-publish). **Partial
   orphan.** A8 covers `DISPUTED` generically but has no requirement gating A1's publish action
   specifically on an open *money* dispute.
3. **A1 BR-150–152 / A3 DEP-303** ("→ A8" — re-trade attempts logged against the carrier, exposed to

   name re-trade frequency as an input signal.
4. **A8 §8.9.4 / A6 §1** (`[NEEDS INPUT]` whether freight charges may be offset against an open cargo
   claim, cross-referenced from both sides). **Not an orphan — flagged so it is not mistaken for one.**
   Both A6 and A8 independently deferred this to counsel; agreement between two domains that a
   question is genuinely open is a different finding than a pointer with no landing page at all, and
   the report distinguishes the two rather than counting this as a fifth orphan.

**Why the count is small relative to ~250 pointers:** the large majority resolved cleanly, either to a
specific named requirement (the common case — the ID-block scheme was built for exactly this) or to a
target domain's general framework (a state, a table, a section) where the source agent's phrasing was
intentionally coarse. Coarse-but-real is not orphaned; the four above are the ones with no landing
point, or a landing point that doesn't close the specific loop the source requirement asserted.

## 3. RSK-308 — the assembler's decision, in full

**Original entry (A3, §4.6):** "RSK-308 | Negligent selection | Price-only award; FMCSA data
available, unused. No preemption shield post-*Montgomery*; the §4.1 gate is the only mitigation left.
| P4 I5" — cross-referenced from A3's own §4.1 (the legal-constraint preamble) and from EC-307 (a tie
where one bidder is worse on safety). A3 explicitly declined to resolve where this belongs, naming the
assembler or A7 as the deciding party, because it is pulled from two directions at once (a design
precondition and an edge case) inside A3's own document.

**A7's challenge (§7, CHALLENGE block):** "Spine §4 treats negligent selection as a consequence to be
'surfaced as a requirement or risk.' That is understated now. *Montgomery* removed the preemption
defence nationwide in May 2026... It is no longer a risk-register line against the lowest-bid rule — it
is a **precondition on the award mechanism itself**."

**Decision: A7's challenge is accepted. RSK-308 is retired as a standalone risk-register entry.**

**Reasoning, in full (reproduced in the BRD at §14.1, summarised here):**

A risk-register entry, by the convention every one of the twelve parts otherwise follows, describes a
probability-weighted outcome that management can consciously accept, mitigate, or transfer — the
register exists to make that choice legible. RSK-308 as A3 originally scoped it ("price-only award with
FMCSA data unused") described exactly that kind of choice: *if* Boss ran an ungated price-only auction,
this was the risk of having done so. But **DEC-LOCK-001**, decided by Boss the same day this document
was assembled, already forecloses that choice — every mechanism option A3 itself presents in §4.7 "runs
on a gated pool," in A3's own words, and "price-only across all comers" is explicitly "no longer a
neutral fork." A risk describing a design choice the design no longer permits is not a residual risk to
be scored and mitigated; it is dead-letter text describing a state the document itself now forbids. Its
substance has migrated from "a risk we might run" to "a precondition already built into every option" —
which is precisely what a precondition is, and is exactly A7's characterisation.

**What happens to the content, so nothing is silently dropped:**

- The precondition itself is retained in full at BRD §8.3 §4.1 (points 1–5), now cross-referenced from
  EC-307 directly (the tie-break edge case) rather than from a risk ID that no longer exists.
- The residual risk that survives *even after* the gate exists — the gate imperfectly specified,
  imperfectly applied, or a genuinely qualified carrier still causes a serious accident — is not the
  same risk as RSK-308 and already had two independent homes before the assembler ever touched it:
  A7's RSK-701 and A8's RSK-801 (plus A8's separate §8.3-narrative framing, tracked as RSK-025 in the
  consolidated register). These are near-duplicates of each other and are cross-referenced against one
  another in the consolidated register (BRD §14.2, RSK-011 and RSK-025) rather than force-merged into
  one ID — the task brief scoped the assembler's explicit merge authority to RSK-308, and a second,
  unrequested merge of A7's and A8's own risk lines was judged out of scope for this pass. It is flagged
  as a recommendation, not executed.

**This is presented in the BRD itself as a finding no single domain agent could see:** three separate
parts (A3, A7, A8) each independently wrote a version of "price-only award is a negligent-selection
risk." Only reading all three together, against DEC-LOCK-001, makes visible that one of the three
(A3's) had already been overtaken by a decision made after A3 drafted, while the other two remained
live.

## 5. Scoping choices made explicit (not silent cuts)

Two deliberate scoping decisions were made to manage length without cutting substance, both stated
inline in `BRD-v1.md` at the point they apply, not buried here:

1. **Full 227-row edge-case inventory not reproduced verbatim in §8.** Each domain's edge cases are
   summarised and the highest-consequence rows cited by ID at point of relevance; the full registers
   remain fully itemised in the twelve source parts (`part-A1-shipper.md` through
   `part-A12-data-licensing.md`), which `BRD-v1.md` §19 Appendices points to directly. Reproducing all
   227 rows a second time in the assembled document was judged to be exactly the padding
   anti-patterns.md warns against, given they already exist at full fidelity one directory reference
   away.
2. **Given/When/Then acceptance criteria (§16) provided in full only for the highest-consequence
   requirements** (the merged BR-303, the eligibility gate, BR-806's rewrite, and A9's BR-900–914 which
   already arrived in that format). The remaining ~200 requirements' Acceptance columns in §8 are
   single-clause, QA-testable conditions — not reformatted into three explicit clauses. This is stated
   as a scoping choice in the BRD itself (§16) and is reflected honestly in the Readiness Report's
   Gherkin-grade sub-score (55/100) rather than claimed as full coverage.

## 6. What no individual domain agent could see (full list)

The task brief asked for this explicitly; consolidated here from where each is also stated inline in
`BRD-v1.md`:

1. **The five-times-asked regulatory-status fork (§3.4 of the BRD).** A6 §1, A7 §7.1, A7 §7.4, A8 §8.1
   `DEC-801`, and A9 §9.2 each independently posed a version of "does the client hold broker authority
   / does the platform touch the money" as a separate `[NEEDS INPUT]`, without cross-referencing each
   other. No single agent, reading only its own domain, could see that these collapse to one decision.
   Consolidated into one blocking-decision table at BRD §13.1.
2. **The RSK-308 / RSK-701 / RSK-801 triangulation (§3 above).** Three agents wrote three versions of
   the same underlying negligent-selection risk from three angles; only reading all three against
   DEC-LOCK-001 together reveals that one had been overtaken by a later decision while the other two
   remained live residual risk.
3. **The DEC-307 / BR-155 / EC-325 triple-ask.** A1 (BR-155) and A3 (DEC-307, and separately EC-325)
   both independently posed "may the shipper decline the lowest qualified bidder" as an open question —
   the same question, asked three times across two documents. Cross-referenced in the assembled BRD
   at §8.1 (BR-155) and §8.3 (DEC-307) so a reader hitting either does not treat it as two separate
   open items.
4. **The three-way independent convergence on "US domestic interstate truckload" (ASM-020/025/040,
   §11).** A7, A8, and A10 each assumed this independently for their own domain's purposes. Read
   together, this is evidence the assumption is probably safe (three independent readings agree) *and*
   a single point of failure (if wrong, three domains' analyses fail simultaneously, not
   independently) — a duality visible only at assembly.
5. **A11's capability-4 and capability-5 licence exposure, which A11 itself did not fully flag.** A11's
   own BR-1108 and BR-1107, read against A12's per-source licence register, describe capabilities that
   partially depend on layers A12 marks "needs a licence decision" (`osm.rest_areas` inside the parking
   capability; the WZDx and Caltrans feeds inside the ETA-credibility capability) — but A11's original
   requirement text did not itself carry the licence caveat, because A11 was drafted with A12's
   findings not yet available for cross-reference. The assembler added the dependency explicitly to
   both amended requirements (BRD §8.10) rather than shipping the capabilities as uniformly available.
6. **The live production exposure at `/v1/fuel` (RSK-046, §14).** A12's own text notes that today's
   `/v1/fuel` endpoint already emits ODbL records directly — the "wrong-hand" pattern under `DEC-1200`
   — a fact stated once, in passing, inside A12's own architecture discussion (§2 of its source part).
   Reading it against the risk register's convention (where it belongs as a scored, named risk with a
   Probability/Impact rating) surfaces it as the single highest-urgency open item in the entire
   document — more urgent than any regulatory `[NEEDS INPUT]`, because it is a *live* exposure in a
   system that already exists, not a future design decision. No individual agent's document format
   forced this comparison; only assembling the risk register in one place did.
