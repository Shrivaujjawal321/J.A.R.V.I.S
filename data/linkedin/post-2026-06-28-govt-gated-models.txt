The biggest AI story this week wasn't a new model. It was who gets to use one.

OpenAI previewed GPT-5.6 "Sol" — stronger at coding, science, and cybersecurity. Then it limited the rollout after a government request, and said publicly this shouldn't become the norm: it "keeps the best tools from users, developers, enterprises, and cyber defenders."

Same week, the US administration cleared Anthropic's Mythos 5 for use across 100+ companies and federal agencies.

Notice the pattern.

For years the gate on a frontier model was compute and red-teaming. Now there's a third gate: government access policy.

What most takes are missing — this changes the builder's risk model, not just the headline:

— The model you architect around today might reach your region on a different timeline than the demo.
— "Available" and "available to you" are now two different questions.
— Capability parity between labs starts to matter less than distribution rights.

If you're building on frontier APIs in 2026, model access is becoming a supply-chain dependency — so plan for it like one. Abstract your provider. Keep a fallback. Don't hard-couple a product roadmap to a model you can't guarantee you'll be allowed to call.
