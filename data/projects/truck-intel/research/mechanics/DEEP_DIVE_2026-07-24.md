# Truck mechanic layer — deep dive, 2026-07-24

Extends `RESEARCH_BRIEF.md` (2026-07-23). That brief was written *before* the
national pull ran. This one is written *after* it, so where the brief estimated,
this measures. Nothing here supersedes the brief's legal rulings (§5) or its
source ledger (§3) — it adds what the ledger could not know until the data landed.

---

## 1. Bottom line

The national pull is **done and loaded**: `core.mechanic_shops` holds **11,759
truck-specific shops**, route-enriched and scored. The brief predicted 10,858
`truck_repair` in a US bbox; the loaded figure is that plus roadside and trailer
categories, so the estimate held.

Three findings change what should be built next:

1. **The verification score is inflated and the reason is measurable.** Every
   single row scores full marks on source agreement, so the signal separates
   nothing. §3.
2. **State licence registries are a verification layer, not a discovery layer.**
   New York alone lists 54,563 repair facilities against 11,759 truck shops
   nationally — but they are all-vehicle registries with no truck flag. §4.
3. **Coverage is not uniform and the thin states are exactly the empty ones.**
   South Dakota has one truck shop per 80 miles of truck route; New Jersey has
   one per 5. A "mechanics near this route" count means something very different
   in Montana than in Ohio. §5.

### Two bugs found and fixed while loading

- **`enrich_routes()` never ran.** `UPDATE … FROM LATERAL` cannot reference the
  update target in PostgreSQL; it raised `InvalidColumnReference` on every run.
  Rewritten to correlate inside its own subquery and join back by `shop_id`. The
  KNN now runs on geometry (indexed) with 10 candidates, then picks the winner by
  true geography distance — ordering by the geography cast directly cannot use the
  index, and ordering by degrees alone picks the wrong segment where a degree of
  longitude is short.
- **`--pull` was the only door to enrichment**, and `--pull` TRUNCATEs. Added
  `--enrich` so route re-assignment does not require re-downloading 11 GB.

## 3. The verification score does not currently discriminate

`source_names` and `n_sources` were meant to carry "how many independent datasets
list this shop". Measured distribution:

| `n_sources` | shops |
|---|---|
| 1 | **0** |
| 2 | 3,824 |
| 3 | 7,935 |

No row has fewer than two. The reason is visible in the source names:

| `source_names` | shops |
|---|---|
| `Overture + Overture-signals + meta` | 5,768 |
| `Overture + meta` | 2,797 |
| `Microsoft + Overture + Overture-signals` | 2,166 |
| `Microsoft + Overture` | 1,027 |

`Overture` and `Overture-signals` are **the same organisation's own pipelines**.
Counting them as two corroborations is double-counting, and 49% of rows
(`Overture + Overture-signals + …`) are scored on exactly that pair. This is the
independence caveat the brief raised in §8.2, now confirmed with numbers rather
than argued in the abstract.

**Consequence:** the 10,794 "verified" figure is not wrong so much as
*uninformative* — the source-agreement component awards full marks to everyone.
The score's real discriminating power comes from the phone/state and coordinate
checks alone.

**Recommended fix:** collapse `Overture*` to one vote before counting, so
`n_independent` becomes 1 for 8,565 rows and 2 for 3,193. Then re-band
`verification_status`. This will move a large share of rows from `verified` to
`probable`, which is the honest outcome, not a regression.

## 4. State licence registries — verification, not discovery

The brief already documented NY (54,563), NJ (1,167) and CT (138). Extending the
survey:

| Source | Rows | Bulk access | Truck-specific? |
|---|---|---|---|
| NY DMV facilities (`data.ny.gov/resource/nhjr-rpi2.json`) | 54,563 | Socrata API, keyless | **No** — all repair classes |
| CA Bureau of Automotive Repair | 34,483 registered dealers (30 Sep 2025) | **No bulk download.** License search + shop locator only; bulk is a Public Records Act request to BAR.PRA@dca.ca.gov | **No** |
| NJ MVC inspection facilities | 1,167 | Socrata, keyless | No |
| CT dealers & repairers | 138 | Socrata, keyless, explicitly `PUBLIC_DOMAIN` | No |
| Montgomery County MD motor vehicle repair & towing | county-scale | Socrata, keyless, updated 2026-05-01 | Partially (towing split out) |

**The pattern is the point.** These registries are large — NY's 54,563 dwarfs the
11,759 national truck shops — but they license *all* vehicle repair. None carries
a "works on Class 8" flag. Using them to discover truck shops would mean
classifying 54,563 auto shops by name, which is exactly the false-positive trap
the brief measured in §6.3.

Their real value is the one the brief already assigned them: an **independent,
governmental existence-and-currency check** — licence number, expiry, county —
that is genuinely independent of Overture, unlike `Overture-signals`. Given §3,
that independence is now the scarcest thing in the verification stack.

**Recommendation:** wire NY + NJ + CT as a *verification* join (name + address
match → `licence_verified`, `licence_expiry`), not as an ingest path. CA is worth
a PRA request only if California coverage later proves thin; at 34,483 all-vehicle
rows it does not solve truck-specificity either.

## 5. Coverage is uneven, and the thin states are the empty ones

Truck-route miles per mechanic shop, states with >1,000 route miles:

| State | Route miles | Shops | Miles per shop |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Dakota | 6,434 | 80 | **80** |
| Montana | 6,721 | 91 | 74 |
| Alaska | 1,082 | 15 | 72 |
| Nebraska | 7,667 | 122 | 63 |
| Mississippi | 6,677 | 108 | 62 |
| Wyoming | 3,911 | 77 | 51 |
| Kansas | 8,912 | 180 | 50 |
| Arkansas | 5,940 | 158 | 38 |
| Oklahoma | 6,812 | 188 | 36 |

This is the number a driver actually feels. On a South Dakota run, "3 mechanics
near this route" is the whole supply for 240 miles. Two readings are possible and
they demand different responses:

- **Real scarcity** — plains states genuinely have fewer heavy-truck shops. Then
  the product should surface *distance to the next shop* prominently, not a count.
- **Data thinness** — Overture under-covers rural business listings. Then a
  second source is needed for exactly these states.

They are separable with the Census CBP denominator the brief already identified
(NAICS 811111 = 84,101 establishments nationally). Per-state CBP counts vs our
per-state counts would show whether SD is empty or merely unlisted. **This is the
single highest-value next measurement** and it needs no new licence, no new
scraping, and no new download — CBP is already in the source ledger.

## 6. Chain networks — small, but the reliable core

| Network | US locations | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Love's Truck Care + Speedco | **400–430** | 1,300+ service bays, 900 roadside service trucks |
| TA / Petro / TA Express | not separately published | the "16,000" figure in their marketing is fuel-card acceptance, not service centres |

At ~430 sites against 11,759 shops, chains are 3.7% of the count but a
disproportionate share of the *dependable* supply: known hours, known
capabilities, national phone support. AllThePlaces (CC0, already in the ledger)
carries chain spiders and is the only permissive source with `opening_hours` —
which is the field a stranded driver at 2 a.m. actually needs and which Overture
does not provide for independents.

**Recommendation:** join AllThePlaces on the chain brands to fill `opening_hours`
and `24h` flags for those ~430 sites, and label them distinctly in the UI. Do not
let them outrank a closer independent shop on distance — rank by distance, badge
by reliability.

## 7. OpenStreetMap — the source we already hold

`data/pbf/us-latest.osm.pbf` (12 GB, ODbL, 2026-07-22) is already on disk and
already feeds `osm.fuel_stations` / `osm.rest_areas` / `osm.weigh_points`. OSM
carries truck-specific repair tagging that Overture does not expose:

- `shop=truck_repair` — the direct equivalent
- `service:vehicle:truck_repair=yes`, `service:vehicle:trailer_repair=yes` —
  capability flags on shops whose primary tag is `shop=car_repair`
- `opening_hours` — the field the permissive sources mostly lack

**The count is not in this revision, and the reason is worth recording.** The
first attempt used a pyosmium `SimpleHandler` with a Python callback on every
node and way. The US extract holds billions of nodes, so that pays Python
interpreter overhead per object and had not finished after 90 minutes while
starving the rest of the pipeline of disk I/O. It was killed, not completed.

The right approach is the one `scripts/osm_extract.py` already uses: a tag filter
applied inside osmium (`.with_filter(...)`) so the C++ layer discards
non-matching objects before Python ever sees them — the same pass that produced
`osm.fuel_stations` in a working job. Adding a `truck_repair` job to that script
is an extension of working machinery, not new machinery, and is the correct next
action rather than another ad-hoc scan.

What is already decided is the **placement**: per the brief's D3 ruling, anything
OSM-derived lands in `osm.*` and is joined at query time, never merged into
`core.*`, so ODbL share-alike never contaminates the permissive core.

**Recommendation:** extend `scripts/osm_extract.py` with a `truck_repair` job
writing `osm.truck_repair`, then expose it in the route corridor as a separate,
clearly-labelled layer alongside `core.mechanic_shops`. Two independent counts —
one permissive, one ODbL — is also the only real cross-check available given §3.

## 8. What remains genuinely unobtainable

Unchanged from the brief §4.3, and worth restating because it is the question
that keeps returning:

- **Ratings, review counts, review text, photos** — no free or paid *legal* path.
  Google's terms forbid it four separate ways; Yelp's robots.txt names AI agents
  explicitly and its API caps storage at 24 hours. Dropped by decision D10, with
  the reason recorded in the schema comment.
- **Live "open now" status** — nothing free publishes it.
- **Actual truck capability** (Class 8 vs light duty, DOT inspection, mobile
  service radius) — inferable from OSM capability tags for a minority; otherwise
  only from the shop's own website text, which is a per-site parse, not a dataset.

## 9. Priority order

1. **Fix the independence count** (§3) — cheap, and it makes every downstream
   confidence number mean something. No new data required.
2. **CBP per-state denominator** (§5) — separates real scarcity from data
   thinness. No new data required.
3. **OSM `shop=truck_repair` extraction** (§7) — the only independent
   truck-specific corroboration available.
4. **AllThePlaces chain hours** (§6) — fills the field drivers need most, for the
   sites most likely to be open.
5. **NY/NJ/CT licence join** (§4) — genuine governmental independence, worth more
   now that §3 has removed the illusion of source agreement.
