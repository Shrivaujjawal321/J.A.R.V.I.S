1. Executive Summary
Two documents were readable for the first time in this round, and they change the review
completely: the Carrier Onboarding workflow (CO-01…CO-28) and the Auth Services
BRD V2 RBAC matrix. Between them they answer four questions that were open in V3 —
and expose one that dwarfs everything found so far.

The headline
    68% of what a carrier enters during onboarding never appears anywhere in the
    Carrier Console.
Onboarding captures 60 business fields across company profile, trucks, trailers, drivers
and yards. 19 of them reach a screen. 41 do not.

This is not a cosmetic gap. A carrier registers a truck's licence plate, plate state and

shows no address at all. A carrier answers "Is the driver insured? Yes/No" (CO-18) — the
exact question the Carrier View BRD raises as unresolvable in OPEN-02.

3. Cross-Document Consistency Review
CD-1 — The role model is now settled, and the Carrier View BRD is the only document
that disagrees
Severity: Critical Affected Module(s): Every module Related BRD(s): Auth Services BRD
V2 (Role Permission Matrix; Functional Matrix); Master Data (User Types per Module);
Carrier Onboarding CO-01…CO-28; Driver BRD §3; Carrier View BRD §2 Related
Screen(s): Entire console; top bar
Observation: Three independent documents now agree on exactly six carrier roles:
   Carrier Super Admin · Fleet Manager · Dispatcher · Compliance Manager · Driver ·
   System Admin
     Auth Services BRD V2 defines all six as matrix columns, with 75 permission codes
     across 18 functional categories, every cell populated.
     Master Data — User Types per Module lists the same six.
     Carrier Onboarding CO-01…CO-28 uses them as actors: Fleet Manager on 8 steps
     (CO-10, CO-12, CO-13, CO-15, CO-16, CO-17, CO-18, CO-22, CO-24), Compliance
     Manager on 2 (CO-07, CO-19), Carrier Super Admin on 4, System Admin on 1.
     Driver BRD §3 independently names Dispatcher and Fleet Manager.

The Carrier View BRD §2 defines two: "Carrier Super Admin" and "Dispatch Admin".
The term "Dispatch Admin" appears in no other document. Fleet Manager and
Compliance Manager appear in none of the Carrier View BRD.
Expected Behaviour: One role model, applied consistently. The evidence is 4-to-1 in favour
of the six-role model.
Business Impact: This was V3's largest open question and it is now answerable. It matters
because Fleet Manager and Compliance Manager do most of the onboarding work —
Fleet Manager owns truck, trailer, driver and device registration; Compliance Manager
owns document upload and driver identity verification. The console has no home for either.
Every permission finding below depends on settling this.
Recommendation: Adopt the six-role model from the Auth Services BRD V2. Update
Carrier View BRD §2 and §17 (which assigns workflows to "Dispatch Admin" throughout).
Rename "Dispatch Admin" → "Dispatcher".

The RBAC matrix gives the Fleet Manager view-only rights over Driver Management and
grants create/update to the Dispatcher. The onboarding workflow makes the Fleet
Manager the creator on all three driver steps.
(For contrast, the two documents agree perfectly on fleet: CO-10/12/13/15/16 name Fleet

compliance: CO-07/CO-19 name Compliance Manager, and RBAC row 10 COMP gives
Compliance Manager Create/View/Update.)
Expected Behaviour: One actor per workflow, consistent across both documents.
Business Impact: Driver onboarding is Critical priority in CO-17 and includes creating an
auth user record. Building it against the wrong role means either the Fleet Manager cannot
do their documented job, or the Dispatcher gets rights the RBAC matrix didn't intend. It
also directly affects CO-22 — the driver-truck mapping — which is the most sensitive item
in the pack (see CD-3).
Recommendation: Reconcile. The pattern elsewhere (Fleet Manager owns assets,
Compliance Manager owns compliance) suggests the RBAC matrix is deliberate and CO-
17/18/22 are the drafting error — but that is inference. Needs Business Clarification (OC-2).



CD-3 — CO-22 "Driver-Truck Mapping" reads as an eligibility roster, not an assignment
— but it is named as an assignment
Severity: Critical Affected Module(s): Driver Management; Fleet Management; Shipment
Execution Related BRD(s): Carrier Onboarding CO-22, CO-23, CO-24; Driver BRD DT-20,
DRV-DASH-001; Fields For HOS (Driver Session); Master Data (Shipment Status) Related
Screen(s): Drivers → Assigned Drivers; Fleet → All Vehicles; Fleet → HOS Status
Observation: CO-22 is titled "Driver-Truck Mapping", its goal is "Assign drivers to trucks",
and it writes to driver_truck_assignments . Under the fixed business model that would be
a direct contradiction.
But its Key Steps say something different:
   "Map Truck VIN → Multiple Drivers (min 4 drivers)" — Acceptance: "minimum 4
   drivers enforced"
A minimum of four drivers per truck cannot be a dispatch assignment. Four people
cannot drive one truck at once. This is a roster — the list of drivers permitted to operate that
truck — not an assignment of a driver to a job.
Read as a roster, CO-22 is fully compatible with the fixed model, and three neighbouring
steps reinforce it:

Expected Behaviour: If CO-22 is a roster, it is the missing authorisation layer — and it
solves the compliance problem in VB-1: truck login can check "is this driver on this truck's
roster, and are they compliant?" If CO-22 is an assignment, it contradicts the fixed model
and must be removed.
Business Impact: This is the single most consequential ambiguity in the pack. The two
readings lead to opposite implementations of the carrier's core operating model. The "min 4
drivers" clause is strong evidence for the roster reading, but the title, goal and table name all
say "assignment".
Recommendation: Needs Business Clarification (OC-1). If roster: rename CO-22 to "Truck
Driver Roster", rename the table, and make it the authorisation source for truck login. If
assignment: it contradicts the fixed model and must be deleted along with the artefacts in
CD-5.

Severity: Medium (downgraded — V3 reported this as a Critical contradiction; the new

Observation: V3 reported Driver BRD DT-09 (Actor: Driver) and AP & AR AP-001 (Actor:
Carrier) as an irreconcilable conflict. The RBAC matrix resolves it:
   Row 6 — POD (Delivery & POD), applies_to: CARRIER Carrier Super Admin: Create /

   Manager, Compliance Manager: no access
And Ledger LED-05 "Delivery Confirmed (POD Uploaded)" — Triggered By: Driver.
So the model is: the Driver captures POD; the Carrier Super Admin may also create or

sequence. The specification is complete and unambiguous once the RBAC matrix is read
alongside it — but no POD screen exists, so a documented Only-View right for the
Dispatcher and a Create/Update right for the Super Admin are both unavailable.
Recommendation: Reword AP-001's actor to "Carrier Super Admin". Build the POD screen
per CG-4.

CD-9 — Per-stop contact details are standard in the Super Set and "PROPOSED" in the
Shipment BRD
Severity: High Affected Module(s): Shipments; Shipment Execution Related BRD(s):
Shipment Execution Super Set (FTL, LTL, Dump Truck sections); Shipment Creation BRD
GAP-10; Carrier View BRD SHP-03 Related Screen(s): Shipments (all buckets)
Observation: The Shipment Execution Super Set carries Pickup Contact Point and
 Delivery Contact Point (e.g. +1-1234567890 ) on every FTL, LTL and Dump Truck
scenario, including one per stop on multi-stop loads.

9. Validation & Business Rule Gaps
VB-1 — The FMCSA compliance gate has no enforcement point under the fixed model
Severity: Critical · Module: Driver Management; Shipment Assignment · BRD: Carrier
View DRV-01 / DRV-002 / FLEET-01; DOC-011 / DOC-017 / DOC-019; CO-20; CO-22; Driver
BRD DT-01; Auth Services BRD (login) · Screens: Drivers → All Drivers; Fleet → All
Vehicles; Shipments
Observation: Carrier View DRV-002 (Critical) blocks a suspended driver at dispatch:
"force SUSPENDED and block dispatch", "Suspended driver cannot be assigned to a trip".
FLEET-01 says allows_dispatch gates dispatch. CO-20 rejects drivers failing the Drug &
Alcohol Clearinghouse check.
Under the fixed model there is no moment at which a driver is dispatched. The carrier
dispatches a truck. The only remaining enforcement point is the driver's login to the truck
— and Driver BRD DT-01's only failure modes are "invalid credentials; session expired".
No CDL check. No medical check. No drug_alcohol_status check. No allows_dispatch
check.
In the console, the suspended driver and the in-maintenance truck render exactly like
every other row — nothing greyed, nothing blocked.
Expected Behaviour: Truck login must validate: driver status ≠
SUSPENDED/TERMINATED; CDL valid; medical certificate valid; drug_alcohol_status
compliant; the truck's allows_dispatch = true ; and — if CD-3 resolves as a roster —
that the driver is on that truck's roster.
Business Impact: The most serious rule failure in this review. The rule is documented in
five places and enforced in none, and the fixed model removed the moment it was designed
to fire. As specified today, a suspended driver logs into a truck and drives away with a live
shipment — a regulatory violation the system is documented to prevent. Note this is a
consequence of a correct business decision that has not been followed through, not a flaw
in the decision.
Recommendation: Move the gate to truck login. Extend DT-01's validations. Rewrite DRV-
002's precondition and acceptance criterion. Add carrier-level suspension (DOC-011). Mark
non-dispatchable assets visibly. This is the second-highest priority in the review.

13. Open Clarifications
Only where the documentation is genuinely insufficient. Nothing below touches
auction/bidding or platform charges, commission, invoicing or payment timing.
OC-1 — Is CO-22 a driver→truck assignment or a truck roster? (Blocks CD-3, CD-5, VB-1)
Its Key Steps say "Map Truck VIN → Multiple Drivers (min 4 drivers)" — four drivers
cannot be assigned to one truck simultaneously, so this reads as an eligibility roster, which
is compatible with the fixed model and would supply the missing login authorisation list.
But its title, goal ("Assign drivers to trucks") and table name all say assignment. The two
readings produce opposite implementations of the core operating model. This is the
single most consequential open question in the review.
OC-2 — Who onboards drivers: Fleet Manager (CO-17/18/22) or Dispatcher (RBAC row
8)? (CD-2) The two in-scope documents disagree directly. The wider pattern (Fleet
Manager owns assets, Compliance Manager owns compliance) suggests RBAC is
deliberate — but that is inference and I will not act on it.
OC-3 — How does a dispatcher account for HOS when selecting an asset? (VB-1) The
carrier picks a truck and cannot know who will log into it. Fleet → HOS Status and Drivers →
HOS become observational, not planning tools. What should happen if the driver who logs
in has insufficient HOS — block the login, warn, alert dispatch, or nothing? No document
addresses this.
OC-4 — Are Shipments and Trips one lifecycle or two? (WF-3) Master Data (16 states) and
the FTL manual (7 states) each model one. The Carrier View BRD models two, unlinked.
This must be answered regardless, because TRIP-01 defines a Trip as "truck + driver +
shipment" and the driver is unknowable at trip creation under the fixed model — the entity
as defined cannot be created.
OC-5 — Should the CO-18 driver-insurance flag become a full policy record? (VB-4) CO-
18 captures "Is the driver insured? Yes/No" and Appendix F contemplates driver-type-
specific insurance. OPEN-02 asked whether per-driver insurance exists — it does, as a flag.
The remaining question is whether it needs policy number, insurer, coverage and expiry.
OC-6 — Is Costco LTL or FTL? (CD-6) The Shipment Execution Super Set gives Costco
 SHP-LTL- prefixes. The Carrier View BRD says Costco reuses SHP-FTL . The Super Set is
the more specific source, but this changes classification, prefix and the field set applied.
OC-7 — Why do Costco scenarios omit contact points? (CD-9) FTL, LTL and Dump Truck
all carry Pickup Contact Point and Delivery Contact Point in the Super Set. Costco
carries none — an inconsistency within a single document.
OC-8 — What is the source of fuel type, GVWR, odometer, yard subtype, geo-fence
radius and CDL endorsements? (DI-2) Six fields the console displays that no onboarding
step captures. Endorsements is the pressing one — Carrier View DRV-07's TSA rule
depends on it and CO-17 never collects it.
OC-9 — Which expiry ladder is authoritative? (CD-8) CO-09's five tiers (1 month → 3
weeks → 2 weeks → 7 days → daily after expiry) vs Notification Types' 30/7. CO-09 is the
more specific and the only one covering post-expiry escalation.
OC-10 — Is "Flat Truckload" a real bucket? (DI-4) It appears in no document other than
the Carrier flow navigation, and currently double-counts flatbed trailers.

Verdict

⚠️ Requires Further Design Review
Not ready for development.
Four reasons, in order:
  1. 68% of what a carrier enters at onboarding never appears in the console. Forty-one
     business fields — licence plate, plate state, registration expiry, yard address, geo-
     coordinates, driver phone, driver email, driver type, EIN, DBA, entity type — are
     captured by a Critical-priority, 28-step workflow and then discarded at the door. Every
     downstream workflow depends on data the console cannot show.
  2. The RBAC matrix is complete and unimplemented. 75 permission codes, 18
     functional categories, 6 carrier roles, every cell populated — and no permission logic
     anywhere. Two of those roles, Fleet Manager and Compliance Manager, perform
     most of the onboarding work and have no console home at all.
  3. The fixed assignment model is contradicted in 22 documented locations, and its
     single most important question — is CO-22 a roster or an assignment? — is
     unanswered. Building against today's documents builds the wrong operating model.
  4. Removing driver assignment removed the FMCSA compliance gate's only trigger,
     and nothing replaced it. As specified, a suspended driver logs into a truck and drives
     away with a live shipment. That is a regulatory exposure created by a correct business
     decision that has not been followed through.


What is genuinely good
Worth saying plainly, because it affects the effort estimate more than the gap list does:
     The onboarding workflow is the strongest document in the pack. CO-01…CO-28 is
     complete, sequenced, actor-assigned, priority-ranked, and carries 28 error/remedy
     pairs. It is ready to build against.
     The RBAC matrix is the most rigorous specification here. 75 permissions × 14 roles,
     fully populated. It needs implementing, not designing.
     The console implements the Carrier View BRD faithfully. Navigation is verbatim. ID
     formats, VIN lengths and trailer lengths all validate. Every open item is flagged on-
     screen rather than papered over.
     The pack already answers four of its own open questions. OPEN-01 → CO-21. OPEN-
     02 → CO-18. OPEN-03 → SHP-RF / SHP-RCR. POD ownership → RBAC row 6 + LED-
     05. These are not gaps — they are cross-references nobody has made yet.
     The fixed assignment model is already half-written into the pack. CO-24 (tablet →
     truck VIN), CO-23 (HOS syncs on tablet login), DT-20 (logout/login handover) and
     DRV-DASH-001 (one current shipment) describe exactly the chosen model. The work
     is deleting the older model that was never removed.

The console is not the problem. It faithfully implements a specification that is itself
incomplete, internally contradictory on roles and assignment, and — most costly of all —
disconnected from the onboarding workflow that feeds it. The expensive work here is
deciding and reconciling, not drawing. Most of what is missing is already specified
somewhere in this pack; it has simply never been pulled into the Carrier View BRD or onto a
screen.
